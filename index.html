03Delhi, Three Ways Monday

You met these two sources in the dossier exemplar. Read them, then predict what each lens would notice before you click.

Slot A · Data

In lockdown Phase 1 (spring 2020), NO₂ in urban areas around Delhi fell by 60% compared with what was expected. Rural areas saw much smaller, short-lived drops.

Misra et al., Scientific Reports, 2021
Slot D · Voices

Karmunisha has sold vegetables from a handcart in Sundar Nagri for nearly 40 years. She and her husband have breathing problems. She was advised to rest, "a luxury daily wagers like her cannot afford."

Down To Earth, 2025
Karmunisha works outdoors all day and lives in a small, poorly ventilated home, so she breathes the worst air at work and at home. The city had more pollution to lose than the countryside.
She was told to rest but can't afford to lose a day's wages. Richer people can stay indoors, work from home or buy purifiers. A cycle: being poor increases her exposure, and the exposure makes her poorer (lost workdays, customers who won't buy).
The satellite study counts molecules, not people. It treats "urban" as one area. In the Down To Earth article a journalist describes her life, and only one sentence is in Karmunisha's own words.
🧭

The exemplar's SQ2 was "Which people in Delhi breathe the most polluted air, and what puts them at greater risk than others?" Which lens is it using?

04Task 1 · The Aral Sea Through a Lens Monday · 10 min

You met the Aral Sea in Week 1. Here's what happened next. In pairs, use the lens your teacher gives you.

  • 1960sSoviet planners divert the Amu Darya and Syr Darya rivers to irrigate cotton.
  • By the 2000sMore than 90% of the sea's surface has dried out. The fish die, fishing towns empty, and toxic salt and dust storms spread illness.
  • 2005Kazakhstan builds the 13 km Kokaral Dam, with World Bank support, to save the North Aral.
  • By 2025North Aral: water volume up 42%, fish catch back to about 8,000 tonnes a year. South Aral (Uzbekistan): still shrinking.
A
Identify who suffered most from the Aral Sea's shrinking.
B
Explain why them, using your lens's question.
C
The North recovered and the South did not. Discuss what that shows about access to solutions.

Environmental equity: where did the cotton profits go? Who lived downstream, by the sea?

Access to solutions: who had the money, water rights and political power to build a fix, and for which part of the sea?

Narratives & voice: the story is often told as "Soviet planners' mistake". Whose experience is missing from that version? (Think fishers, farmers, and families in Karakalpakstan.)

Facts: Global Voices (May 2026); World Bank, "Saving a Corner of the Aral Sea" (2005).

06Task 3 · Interview Questions Tuesday · 12 min

Slot E is optional, but everyone drafts questions today. Writing good open questions is a research skill. An interview can give you a real voice and local detail. It can't tell you about everyone, and it can't replace Slots A–D.

Warm up: open or closed?

Closed questions get "yes", "no" or one word. Open questions get stories and reasons.

Who could you ask?

🐠 Coral reefs

A diver or dive instructor; someone with family in a reef country.

🌾 The Sahel

Someone with family in West Africa; an aid or development worker.

🏔️ Glaciers & Arctic

A glacier guide or hiker; someone from Northern Norway or a mountain region.

Now draft yours

1
Decide who you could realistically ask. No one yet? Write for an imagined expert, as practice.
2
Write 3–5 open questions (Who / How / Why). At least one must connect to your new SQ2.
3
Test each one: could Google answer it? Could it be answered in one word? Does it suggest an answer? Rewrite it.
4
Paste them into Slot E, or into your notes if you're not doing Slot E.

07Interview Rules Tuesday

Ask first
Check with your teacher before you contact anyone.
Ask permission
Explain what it is for and that they can stop at any time.
Record it
Write down who, when, and how (in person or by message).
Quote accurately
Use their exact words, or say clearly when you're summarising.
Protect them
No full name without permission. "A dive instructor in Stavanger" is fine.
🛡️

Family, family friends and school staff are the safest people to ask. Don't contact strangers online.
